Clean and dirty priceThe dirty price is what a buyer actually pays: the bond's value including interest built up since the last coupon. The clean price strips that accrued interest out, so it doesn't jump on coupon dates. Bonds are quoted clean.

Market maker

i
A simulated bot that quotes a bid and an ask on each bond around its own fair value, within limits stored in the database. Clients in a simulated market trade with it: uninformed ones at random, informed ones only when its quote is stale. Read-only. Pause, resume, the kill switch, limit changes, runs and unwinds are done with scripts/mm.py, each with who and why, and audited. Reload the page to see a change.

Simulated: not evidence of real profitability. The market, the clients and their behaviour are all modelled. A positive result here says the model is self-consistent, not that the strategy would make money.

Live market data, read-only. Real prices from validated ledgers, not a model and not a bond price: no bond value is used for this pair. Nothing here trades; paper trading comes later in M7, and anything live only after Ethan's explicit approval (M8).

Risk

What a rate move does to each bond
i
Each bond is repriced in full after every shock. The duration-plus-convexity estimate sits beside it, so you can see where the quick approximation stops working. Duration in one line Modified duration is the approximate % price change for a 1 percentage point yield move. A duration of 14 means a 1 point rise in yield costs roughly 14% of the price. Convexity is the correction for the fact that this relationship curves.
How much the price depends on the long-end rule
i
No government curve reaches 100 years, so rates past its last point are a modelling choice, not an observation. Each rule below holds the forward rate constant beyond that point; only its level differs. Every published rate is identical across rows, so the spread of prices is pure model risk.
Price change for a parallel yield move
i
Clean price change against a change in each bond's own yield. The curvature is convexity. Why prices fall when yields rise A bond's payments are fixed. Its price is what those payments are worth today, discounted at current rates. Higher rates shrink every future payment's value, and the further away a payment is, the more it shrinks. That is why the 100-year bond moves so much more than the 3-year one.
