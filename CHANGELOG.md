- **Simulated market (M4 step 5).** `debtlab/marketmaker/simulation.py`, migration `010_mm_simulation.sql` (`mm_runs`, `mm_fills`, partial fills on `mm_orders`). A run is a day of one-minute steps on a simulated clock. The government curve moves as a seeded random walk with occasional 8 bp jumps, on top of the real curve; the bot sees moves 3 steps late and requotes every 10 steps through the usual quote decisions and execution check (cancelling its old quotes first). **Uninformed clients are price-sensitive**: they deal with probability exp(−k × distance of the quote from fair value in bp) (Ethan's change 1). **Informed clients** know the true value and take a stale quote in full: adverse selection. Every fill is a ledger trade plus a 0.5 bp fee; an optional end-of-run unwind sells inventory above target to an outside market at its bid less impact: slippage. Everything is labelled SIMULATED.
- **P&L split, moved forward from step 6** because Ethan asked for P&L in this step's comparison: `debtlab/marketmaker/pnl.py`. Spread captured + market move on the starting position + MTM on traded inventory − fees − slippage, per currency, at the bot's own marks, with the total also worked out from cash and inventory alone; the two agree exactly (tested by hand and on runs with slippage).
- **Calibration profiles** (Ethan's change 1): *normal* (jumps 0.2% a step, ≈ 8.1 bp daily yield s.d., close to real 30-year gilts) and *stress* (1% a step, ≈ 16.3 bp). `daily_sd_bp` reports what a profile implies; a test checks 400 simulated days against it.
- **Markouts** at 5 and 30 steps per fill, by client type, pulled forward from step 6.
- **Unwind after a kill only on command** (Ethan's change 3): the end-of-run unwind is skipped when the bot is killed and the summary says so; `simulation.unwind` does it explicitly, with who and why.
- **Informed clients arrive on top of the uninformed ones** (`informed_arrival_prob`, its own random stream; replaces `informed_share`), so the gap between runs with and without them is adverse selection, not lost volume (Ethan's change). Both kinds can fill in one step, under their own fill ids.
- **Many-seed comparison**: `scripts/mm_seeds.py` (Ethan's change 2), parallel, every run checked for the identity and ledger integrity; results in `results/mm_seeds_2026-10-30.json`, table in ROADMAP.
- Same seed, same run. A run that crashes part-way is finished by running it again: each step's quotes and fills commit together, the run continues from the next step, and the result is identical to an uninterrupted run.
- **Fix: a daily-loss breach now pauses the whole bot before any quoting** (`execution.enforce_daily_loss`, called by every quoting round and every order), as approved in decision 3. Before, step 4's quote decision dropped only that currency's quotes, so no order was ever submitted and the pause never fired; the step 4 test that expected that is corrected.
- **Quote nothing when invalid (M4 step 4).** `debtlab/marketmaker/quoting.py` decides each round's quotes and records every decision, quote or not, in `mm_quotes` (migration 009) with its reasons, spread components, data timestamps, inventory, sizes, spread in currency and bp, and the limits in force. Nothing is quoted when: the bot isn't running; there is no market maker in the database; the ledger fails its integrity check; the curve is missing, illustrative or stale; the bond can't be valued; it is halted (defaulted, matured, outside its life); or it's the fund share (never quoted off NAV). A side is dropped, with the reason, when it fails the execution check (cash reserve, inventory, exposure, daily loss, open orders) or the bot can't honour it (no units to sell). Sides decided in the same round count as open orders; an instrument's own open orders count as replaced. If the market maker holds a bond it can't value, it makes no bid in that bond's currency.
- **Restricted quoting** (grace period, decision 4): only the ask, at twice the spread (a recorded *restricted* component equal to the rest) and half the size, priced flat on the distressed basis with DV01 from a 1 bp curve shift.
- **Gap closed:** the quote model takes maximum inventory from the database limit in force (`controls.limit`), never from config; `load_quote_params` now requires it. Only `seed_limits` reads the config's starting values.
- `execution.check` takes `pending` (orders about to be placed) and `replacing` (open orders about to be cancelled).

- **Limits, pause and kill switch (M4 step 3).** `debtlab/marketmaker/controls.py` and `execution.py`, migration `008_mm_controls.sql`. Limits and the bot's state are stored append-only (`mm_limit_changes`, `mm_state_changes`): the latest row is in force, every change keeps who and why and is audited. The 14 approved limits are copied from `config/marketmaker.json` the first time only; after that the database rules, so neither a restart nor an edited config loosens a limit. Every order goes through one check, `execution.check`, which reads limits and state from the database each time, assumes every open order on the same side fills, and lists every limit broken: state, order size, open orders, data age, daily loss, inventory (with open bids), no selling beyond holdings (with open offers), market value and DV01 per currency, cash reserve (with open bids). Refusals are audited with their reasons; a missing limit blocks rather than allows.

- **Quote model (M4 step 2).** `debtlab/marketmaker/quotes.py`, pure functions. A quote starts at fair value (clean per 100), is skewed against inventory (long → both prices lower), and gets a half-spread that is the sum of eight recorded components, each with its size in price and bp and its reason: base cost, duration (assumed yield move × DV01), credit, data age, inventory imbalance, illiquidity, fees and long-end model risk (century only: 0.5 × half the ±50 bp long-run-forward band). Prices are rounded outward to the 0.01 tick; sizes come in 1,000s with a 10,000 minimum; no ask without inventory (no short sales), no bid at maximum inventory. Each quote records the curve id, observation date, fetch time and age, the settlement date, inventory and its limits, and the full long-end range as context. Parameters in `config/marketmaker.json`. Nothing quotes yet: steps 3–5 decide when.

- **Trades with a cash leg (M4 step 1).** `ledger.trade()` books delivery versus payment: units to the buyer and cash to the seller in one transaction, all or nothing, idempotent by trade id. Quotes are clean prices; the buyer pays **clean + accrued**, except on a bond in grace period or default, which **trades flat** (Ethan's change). Settles on the trade date; cash rounded to the cent. Example: $100,000 of the short bond at 97.25 on 30 Oct 2026 costs $98,162.50 (accrued 0.9125); in grace at 88.50 it costs $88,500.
- **Risk R15 fixed.** The ledger now refuses any unit movement in a bond dated after its final record date (short bond: 2 Feb 2029), whether or not principal has been scheduled; only redemptions are allowed after it. Each bond's final record date is stored in `bond_terms` when the ledger is seeded. The M3 refusal when scheduling principal stays as a second line of defence.
- **Market maker's book.** Three new parties: *Market maker (simulated)*, *Simulated market (clients)*, the other side of every client order, and *Funding (outside the simulation)*, the only party besides issuers that may go negative, and in cash only. Units come from a simulated tap issue of each bond on its issue date (`config/marketmaker.json`): market maker £1m century and $1m short, market £2m and $2m; cash £1.5m and $1.5m for the market maker, £5m and $5m for the market. Neither trading party can go below zero, so nobody sells short. The app seeds the book in the main database. Scenario databases get it only with `new_scenario_db.py --market-maker`, so M3's lifecycle figures are unchanged without it.
- Migration `007_market_parties.sql`: `parties` gains the roles *market_maker*, *market* and *funding* (rebuilt, since SQLite can't change a CHECK), and the new table `bond_terms`. Other tables reference `parties`, so the migration runner now supports a migration marked `-- foreign-keys: off`: foreign keys off for the rebuild, every reference checked before committing, then back on. The scenario starts a **simulation date** on the day it is created; it only moves forward. A coupon is **scheduled** first, fixing who is owed what from holdings at the end of the **record date** (short bond 15 calendar days before payment, century bond 1 business day before; set per bond in `config/instruments.json`), then **paid** one holder at a time, each holder's ledger transaction and *paid* mark committed together. A run only pays what is still due, so a crash part-way is finished by running again and nobody is paid twice. Dana's amounts are recorded as unpaid (compliance hold). Per-holder cash is rounded to cents; the rounding residue is stored. Scheduling closes the books: the ledger then refuses movements in that bond dated on or before the record date.
