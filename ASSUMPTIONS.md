## Lifecycle (scenario database only)

| Item | Assumption | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Simulation date | Starts on the day the scenario database is created, stored in `db_meta`, only moves forward, advanced by hand. Same ledger plus same date gives the same result. | Deterministic, repeatable demos. |
| Record date | Counted back from the **scheduled** payment date, before any weekend roll. Short USD bond: 15 calendar days before. Century GBP bond: 1 business day before (weekends only; no bank holidays). Per instrument in `config/instruments.json`. Holders at the end of the record date are paid. Example: 17 Feb 2029 is a Saturday, so the payment moves to Monday 19 Feb, but the record date stays 2 Feb. | US indentures count from the scheduled date (Ethan, 30 Oct; step 4 had counted from the rolled date, giving 4 Feb). Same rule for both bonds for consistency; for the century bond the two methods only differ when a payment date falls on a weekend. |
| Books close at the record date | Once a payment is scheduled, the ledger refuses movements in that bond dated on or before its record date. | Otherwise a back-dated transfer could change who "should" have been paid after the fact. |
| Entitlement | Units × coupon per 100 ÷ 100, rounded to cents per holder (banker's rounding). The exact total minus the rounded total is stored as the rounding residue. | Cash settles in cents; the residue makes rounding visible. |
| Payment date in the ledger | A coupon's ledger transaction is dated its scheduled payment date (after the weekend roll), even if the run happens later. | The coupon belongs to that date; late payment of an old coupon isn't modelled yet. |
| Due payments | Every coupon whose payment date is on or before the simulation date, oldest first, then principal on the maturity payment date (after the final coupon). | |
| Principal | Paid like a coupon: same record date, one atomic block per holder. Each holder's transaction pays face in cash *and* hands the units back to the issuer, so cash and units move together. | A redemption is an exchange; recording one leg without the other would leave the ledger wrong after a crash. |
| Maturity status | *Matured* once every principal entitlement is paid or recorded unpaid, dated the principal payment date, set once. Anything unpaid stays recorded as owed. Dana's units stay outstanding (the issuer still owes her $1m) until she is paid. | The brief: settle principal once, change status, record anything unpaid. |
| After the final record date | The ledger refuses any unit movement in a bond dated after its final record date (short bond: 2 Feb 2029), except redemptions, whether or not principal has been scheduled. Each bond's final record date is stored in `bond_terms` when the ledger is seeded. If units had moved anyway (a database without `bond_terms`), scheduling principal is still refused. | Principal is paid to, and redeemed from, the holders on the final record date, so they must still hold the units. Fixes risk R15 (M4 step 1); before it, a late transfer stopped the scenario. |
| Blocked holder | Dana's entitlement is recorded as *unpaid (payments blocked)* when the coupon is scheduled; nothing is posted. The coupon's status is then *partly paid*. A hold can't be lifted yet. | Gives "unpaid amounts" a concrete case outside default. |
| Payment status | *scheduled* → *in progress* → *paid* (all holders) or *partly paid* (some still unpaid); *missed* when the issuer pays nobody (default scenario). | |
| Instrument status | `instrument_status` (migration 005, widened in 006): no row means *performing*; then *grace_period*, *defaulted* or *matured*. Moved from migration 004 to 005, since step 4 is the first to write it. | No unused table. |

## Default scenario (scenario database only)

Terms approved by Ethan on 30 Oct 2026, with his two corrections (recovery on the whole claim; trading flat). They are stored with each default when it is triggered.

| Item | Assumption | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Trigger | By hand (`lifecycle.py default SHORT-USD-2029`), on a performing bond with a coupon still to come. The **next coupon after the simulation date** is missed. Payments due before it are paid normally. | The brief: one bond, triggered manually. |
| Missed coupon | Scheduled as usual (record date, entitlements), then every entitlement is recorded *unpaid, issuer missed payment*; the payment is *missed*; nothing is posted to the ledger. The bond enters a **grace period**. | |
| Grace period | 30 days. The issuer can't cure it in this simulation, so default is declared on day 30 (17 Feb 2027 → 19 Mar 2027). | Typical bond indenture grace for interest. |
| After the missed coupon | No later coupon or principal is scheduled or paid. The claim is settled by the recovery alone. | Acceleration: after default, holders claim face plus unpaid interest rather than the original schedule. |
| Trades flat | From the missed coupon on, the bond **trades flat**: no accrued interest, so clean = dirty. The claim to the missed coupon goes with the bond to whoever holds it, and the price includes it. | Market convention for defaulted bonds (Ethan's correction, 30 Oct). |
| Price in grace | Remaining scheduled payments discounted at government zero rate + **1,000 bp** distressed spread, plus the missed coupon at face (owed now). Quoting *restricted* (M4 defines what that allows). | |
| Price after declaration | The recovery value of the claim: **40% × (face + missed coupons)** = 40% × 102.25 = **40.90 per 100**, discounted from the recovery payment date to the simulation date at government zero rate + 1,000 bp. Quoting *halted*. Not 40: the claim includes the missed coupon (Ethan's correction, 30 Oct). | The 40% is an assumption about outcome; the distressed spread prices the time and uncertainty until it arrives. |
| Recovery | 12 months after declaration (19 Mar 2028, a Sunday, paid Monday 20 Mar), 40.90 per 100 to the holders at its record date (15 days before 19 Mar: 4 Mar 2028). Each holder's transaction pays cash and returns the units together, like principal. | Recovery takes time; 12 months is short for a real workout. |
| Missed coupon after the recovery | Written off: the recovery is a share of the whole claim, missed coupon included, so the coupon isn't owed on top. Before the recovery is scheduled it shows as owed. | Otherwise it would be counted twice. |
| Per-holder loss | Claim (units × 102.25 / 100) minus the recovery entitlement. With no trading after the default, every holder loses 60% (Alder: claim $4,090,000, recovered $1,636,000, loss $2,454,000). Dana's recovery is owed but unpaid (hold), and shown as owed, not as a loss. | |
| Repricing at a future date | Today's curve, held constant: times are measured from the simulation date. The CLI uses the scenario database's latest stored official curve, else the illustrative one, labelled. | No forecast of future curves. |

## Market maker (M4)

| Item | Assumption | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Parties | *Market maker (simulated)* (`market-maker`) quotes from its own account. *Simulated market (clients)* (`market`) is the other side of every client order. *Funding (outside the simulation)* (`funding`) supplies the starting cash. All fictional. | Decision 1, approved by Ethan on 30 Oct 2026. The four test holders don't trade, so the lifecycle demos are unchanged. |
| Who may go negative | Issuers (units issued, cash paid out) and the funding account, in cash only. The market maker and the market can't go below zero in anything: no short selling. | The market maker can only sell what it holds, which also bounds its inventory below. |
| Starting book | Simulated tap issue of each bond on its issue date: market maker £1,000,000 century and $1,000,000 short; market £2,000,000 and $2,000,000. Cash on 2 Jan 2026: market maker £1,500,000 and $1,500,000, market £5,000,000 and $5,000,000. In `config/marketmaker.json`. | Approved by Ethan on 30 Oct 2026. About one point of inventory either side of target (£1m of the century bond is about £1,370 per bp), cash enough to buy up to the inventory limit and keep a reserve; the market is large enough never to run out within a session. |
| Tap issue | Units outstanding grow by the tap (short bond $10m → $13m in the main database). | Units can only come from the issuer; taking them from the test holders would change their positions. |
| Where the book is | Seeded on start in the main database. A scenario database gets it only when created with `new_scenario_db.py --market-maker`; there the market maker and the market are holders of record and are paid like anyone. | Keeps M3's lifecycle figures for a scenario made without it. |
| Trades | Delivery versus payment in one ledger transaction (`ledger.trade`), idempotent by trade id. Quotes are clean prices per 100. Cash = face × (clean + accrued) / 100, rounded to the cent (banker's rounding). A bond in grace period or default trades flat: no accrued. Settles on the trade date. | Ethan's change 2 (30 Oct). Trading flat as in the default scenario. No T+1/T+2 lag yet, as for pricing. |
| No trading after the final record date | Same rule as for transfers (see Lifecycle). | Fixes R15. |
| Limits (used from step 3) | Per currency, no FX (decision 3). Maximum inventory £2,000,000 / $2,000,000 face (floor 0: no shorts); portfolio exposure £2,000,000 and £3,000 per bp / $2,000,000 and $450 per bp; maximum order 250,000 face; daily loss £20,000 / $5,000; at most 4 open orders; maximum data age 96 hours; minimum cash reserve £250,000 / $250,000. | Approved by Ethan on 30 Oct 2026. Sized from the real 29 Oct DV01s: £1m of the century bond ≈ £1,370 per bp, $1m of the short bond ≈ $209 per bp. Daily loss ≈ a 15 bp move on £1m century, 24 bp on $1m short. |
| Limits in the database | Append-only; the latest value is in force. Copied from config the first time each limit is missing, never again, so a restart or an edited config can't loosen one. Changed only with who and why. A limit that isn't set blocks orders. | The brief: limits persisted so a restart can't bypass them. |
| Bot state | *running*, *paused* or *killed*; a database where the bot has never run reads as paused. Pausing cancels open orders. The kill switch cancels them and stays on until a reset with a reason, which leaves the bot paused, not running. | Starting and restarting should be deliberate. Approved by Ethan on 30 Oct 2026. |
| The check | One function, before any order: reads limits and state fresh each time; assumes every open order on the same side fills (inventory, exposure and cash count open bids, selling counts open offers); offers never count towards exposure, since they can only reduce it; data age is the age of that instrument's own curve; lists every breach. | Worst case, so a burst of fills can't take the bot past a limit. Offers not counted towards exposure: approved by Ethan on 30 Oct 2026. |
| Daily loss | Per currency. A breach in either currency pauses the whole bot and cancels every order, checked before every quoting round and every order. **The day's P&L is worked out from the ledger** (`daily.daily_pnl`, M4 step 6), never passed in: the day is the UTC calendar day of the clock (simulated in a run); the market maker's cash, units and marks are fixed as the day's open the first time it is needed (`mm_day_open`); then P&L = change in cash + units now × mark now − units at open × mark at open, less money or units put in from outside (`funding`, `issue`). Fees and coupons need no special case. A holding that can't be valued now makes its currency's P&L unknown and bids in that currency are refused; one unvalued at the open gets its opening mark the first time it is valued (marked late, audited). Marks are the bot's own: inside a run, the run's simulated curve; outside, the real stored curve. **So after a run, `status` shows the day's P&L at the real curve, without the run's simulated market move** (that move isn't stored anywhere). | Simplest safe reaction; resuming is a deliberate act. Approved by Ethan on 30 Oct 2026. Ledger-based P&L closes the gap where a caller could hide a loss from the limit. | Simplest safe reaction; resuming is a deliberate act. Approved by Ethan on 30 Oct 2026. |
| Restricted quoting | A bond in grace period: only the ask (selling is the only side that reduces inventory, since nobody sells short), half-spread doubled by a recorded *restricted* component, half the quote size. Fair value from `scenario_price` (flat, distressed spread); DV01 from repricing on a curve 1 bp higher; credit component on the distressed spread; no long-end component. | Decision 4, approved by Ethan on 30 Oct 2026. |
| Unvalued holdings | If the market maker holds a bond that couldn't be valued (no curve, valuation failed), its exposure is unknown, so no bid is made in that bond's currency; if the bond isn't configured at all, no bid in any currency. Offers are still allowed. | Exposure limits can't be checked against a position with no value. Approved by Ethan on 30 Oct 2026. |
| Quote records | Every decision is stored in `mm_quotes`, including "none", with reasons. The quote in force for limits and orders is decided per round; placing it is step 5. | The brief: every quote records its fair value, prices, spread, data timestamps, inventory, limits and reasons. |
| Simulated market | One run = 390 one-minute steps. Benchmark: normal moves of 0.2 bp a step plus a chance each step of an 8 bp jump, either way, per currency, seeded. **Two named profiles** (Ethan's change 1): *normal*, jump chance 0.2% a step, implying a daily yield s.d. of √(390 × (0.2² + 0.002 × 8²)) ≈ 8.1 bp, close to real 30-year gilts; *stress*, 1% a step, ≈ 16.3 bp, for showing adverse selection (diffusion alone: ≈ 3.9 bp). Normal unless asked. Bot sees moves 3 steps late, requotes every 10 steps (pulling every old quote first). An uninformed client arrives for each bond with 15% chance a step; size random in 1,000s from the minimum order to the quote size; random side; deals with probability exp(−k × distance in bp of price). **Informed clients arrive on top**, with their own chance a step (0 by default; 4.5%, a third of the uninformed rate, in the comparison) and their own random stream, so adding them leaves the uninformed flow unchanged and the difference is adverse selection, not lost volume (Ethan's change). An informed client deals only when the true value is beyond the bot's quote, for everything offered, and goes first in a step; an uninformed client in the same step deals on what is left. Fees 0.5 bp of cash per fill, paid to the funding account (outside the simulation). | Approved by Ethan on 30 Oct 2026 with the calibration change. Jumps stand for news the bot learns late. |
| Informed flow is random, not news-driven | Informed clients arrive at random times, independent of the jumps. In real markets informed flow clusters around news, exactly when quotes are most stale, **so the simulated adverse selection is likely understated** compared with real markets. | Added at Ethan's request, 30 Oct 2026. |
| Unwind | Optional at the end of a run: inventory above target is sold to an outside market at true value less its half-spread (century 50 bp, short 5 bp of price) and 10 bp per million face of impact, rounded down to the tick. Runs automatically when the bot is running or paused; **after a kill, never automatically, only on an explicit command** (`simulation.unwind`, with who and why; latest finished run only, once; it doesn't revive the bot) (Ethan's change 3). | Approved by Ethan on 30 Oct 2026. |
| P&L split | Per currency, dirty values, bot's own marks (no market price exists). Spread captured: each client fill's cash against size × the bot's fair at the fill. Slippage: the same on unwinds, as a cost. MTM in two lines (Ethan's change 2): **market move on the starting position** = starting units × mark change; **MTM on traded inventory** = each fill's signed units × (end mark − fair at the fill). Total also from cash and inventory alone; the identity is exact. The loss limit doesn't use this split: it reads the day's P&L from the ledger (see Daily loss), which on a fresh day agrees with the run's total to within a penny. | Ethan's changes. |
| Markouts | Per fill (unwinds excluded), at 5 and 30 steps (capped at the run's last step), against the bot's own fair value then: buying, size × fair / 100 − cash; selling, cash − size × fair / 100. Summed per currency and client type. | Measures adverse selection with what the bot itself can see. |
| Many-seed comparison | `scripts/mm_seeds.py`: each run on its own in-memory copy of the main database (never changed), set up as the app would, bot running, latest official curves. Every run checked: identity exact, ledger integrity ok. Results kept in `results/` with the curve ids used. | Ethan's change 2. |
| Simulated runs and replay | All randomness is drawn from (seed, step, instrument), so the same seed gives the same run. Each step commits as a whole; a rerun continues after the last committed step. A run id can't be reused with other parameters. | Crash recovery like the lifecycle's. |
| Tick and sizes | Tick 0.01 per 100; minimum order 10,000 face, sizes in steps of 1,000; quote size 100,000 face. | Approved by Ethan on 30 Oct 2026. |
| Fill-rate k (used from step 5) | Probability an uninformed client fills = exp(−k × half-spread in bp of price). Century 0.02 per bp (37% at 50 bp, 14% at 100 bp); short 0.10 per bp (61% at 5 bp, 37% at 10 bp). | Ethan's change 1 and approved values (30 Oct). Without it a wider spread costs nothing in fills. The century's natural spread is about ten times wider, so it needs a smaller k. |
| Quote model | Quotes are clean prices per 100. Centre = fair value − skew; skew = DV01 × 3 bp × imbalance, where imbalance runs from −1 (no inventory) through 0 (at the 1m target) to +1 (at the 2m limit). Half-spread = sum of components: **base** 1 bp of price; **duration** DV01 × 1 bp (yield move before the bot can react); **credit** DV01 × 0.5% of the credit spread (century 0.475 bp, short 0.55 bp); **data age** DV01 × 0.5 bp per day of curve age; **inventory** DV01 × 2 bp × \|imbalance\|; **illiquidity** 10 bp of price for the century bond, 2 bp for the short; **fees** 0.5 bp of the cash per fill; **long-end** as below. Bid rounded down and ask up to the tick. | Approved by Ethan on 30 Oct 2026. Expressing risks as yield moves × DV01 makes the century bond wider for the same risk, as it should be. Sized so the half-spreads (century ≈ 79 bp, short ≈ 8 bp on the real 29 Oct curves) give uninformed fill rates of about 21% and 43% with the approved k. |
| Century long-end add-on (used from step 2) | Weight 0.5 × half the price gap between the "long-run forward −50 bp" and "+50 bp" cases. On the real 29 Oct curve: 0.5 × (91.2748 − 90.0543) / 2 = 0.31 per 100, about 34 bp of the price. The full extrapolation range (89.56 to 94.11) is shown as context only. | Decision 2 and the weight approved by Ethan on 30 Oct 2026. |

## Pricing

| Item | Assumption |
|---|---|
| Curve rates | Each curve states its compounding. US Treasury: par yields bootstrapped to **semi-annual zero rates**. Bank of England: **continuously compounded** spot rates (stated in the BoE yield-curve FAQ). ECB: treated as **continuously compounded** (the ECB data portal labels the series this way; full documentation not yet read). Illustrative curves: the bond's coupon frequency. |
| Credit spread compounding | The spread is added to the zero rate and compounded the same way, so on gilt curves it is a continuously compounded spread. |
| Gilt curve | BoE nominal spot curve, 0.5–40 years every 6 months; the latest day with at least 20 maturities is used. Beyond 40 years the rate is held flat. |
| Treasury bootstrap | Semi-annual grid out to 30 years; par yields linearly interpolated between published maturities; at 6 months and shorter, zero = par (no intermediate coupons). Grid times are exact half-years, while the pricer measures time as ACT/365F, a difference of a few days at most. |
| Interpolation | Linear between curve points. |
| Long end | **Flat extrapolation** of the zero rate beyond the curve's last tenor (the price used everywhere). For the century bond on a 40-year gilt curve, about 8% of value depends on this. |
| Long-end sensitivity | Shown, not used for pricing. Every rule holds the forward rate constant past the last tenor and differs only in its level: the 40Y zero rate (current rule); the last-segment forward (39.5–40Y); the 30–40Y average forward; and the 40Y zero rate ±50/±100 bp. The offsets move the *forward*, not the zero rate, so the curve joins smoothly; a zero-rate jump at 40Y would re-discount a 40.5-year payment over its whole life and overstate the effect. On the real 29 Oct gilt curve the long end falls, so its forwards (3.28%, 4.20%) sit well below the 40Y zero rate (5.33%) and give prices 1.8–3.8% higher. The last-segment forward rests on two neighbouring points of the BoE's fitted curve and is noisy. |
| Discounting time | ACT/365F years from settlement to payment date. |
| Yield, duration, convexity | Street convention: periods counted with the bond's day count, compounded at coupon frequency. Matches QuantLib. |
| Payment dates | Rolled to the next weekday. Bank holidays are not modelled yet. |
| Accrual dates | Unadjusted (standard for bonds). |
| Settlement | Today in UTC by default; set `DEBTLAB_SETTLEMENT_DATE` to override. No T+1/T+2 settlement lag yet. |
| Stubs | Short first periods handled under ACT/ACT (ICMA) and 30/360. Long stubs aren't generated. A first period is regular when it starts exactly one period before its end *as measured from maturity*, so a bond issued 31 Aug and maturing 31 Aug has a regular first period ending 28 Feb. |
| Regular coupons | Every regular period pays exactly rate ÷ frequency (3.00 per 100 for 6% semi-annual), whatever the day count, as bond indentures specify. QuantLib's `FixedRateBond` on 30/360 instead sets month-end coupons by day count (2.9667, 3.05, …), so that case is not compared with QuantLib. 30/360 is bond basis with no end-of-February adjustment, so yield exponents of month-end 30/360 bonds differ from whole periods by a day or two. None of our instruments is month-end. |

## Data

| Item | Assumption |
|---|---|
| Source failures | Any failure loading a currency's curve (download, parse, bootstrap) is recorded with its type and falls back to that currency's last stored official curve; other currencies load normally. The dashboard banner lists each failure from the last refresh. In offline mode no source is contacted, and the banner says offline mode was chosen rather than that anything failed. |
| Bonds outside their life | A bond is not priced before its issue date or on/after maturity; it shows "not priced" with the reason and the other bonds are unaffected. |
| Freshness | A daily curve becomes **stale after 96 hours**, measured from midnight UTC on its observation date. That allows a weekend plus one late publication. |
| Illustrative GBP curve | 10Y and 30Y set near levels reported in the press on 14 Oct 2026 (10Y about 5.4%, 30Y about 6%). Every other point is invented for shape. |
| Illustrative USD curve | Entirely invented for a plausible shape. |
| Source formats | Treasury, ECB and BoE parsers verified against real downloads on 30 Oct 2026 (saved in `tests/fixtures/real/`). |
